# Technical Language Principles

Use this reference for broad editorial work or questions about terminology, modality, and evidence. Follow project-specific audience, vocabulary, and voice first.

## Meaning and Reader Intent

Put the reader's goal before background. Use concrete verbs and familiar language; define domain terms when readers need them. Keep one term for each concept and preserve exact names, commands, paths, interface labels, and API identifiers.

Prefer active voice when responsibility matters. Keep qualifications near the claim or action they constrain. Split dense sentences when they combine separate requirements, but do not impose sentence-length quotas or mechanical word substitutions.

Use clear, respectful language for the actual audience. Avoid cultural assumptions, needless jargon, and wording that minimizes the reader's work. Preserve deliberate product or community voice when it adds meaning.

House-language defaults: prefer `for example` and `that is` over `e.g.` and `i.e.`, and a bounded example or clear category over `etc.`. Prefer `conventional` or `readable` over `boring`, `unrelated change` over `drive-by change`, and `authoritative source` or `canonical home` over `source of truth` when those terms fit. Replace vague `surface` with the actual interface, document, workflow, or boundary unless it is an established domain term. Apply these choices in context, preserving quotations, exact names, and deliberate local voice.

## Requirements and Evidence

Preserve normative force: `must` or an imperative expresses a requirement, `prefer` a default, `can` capability, and `might` possibility. Do not mechanically replace `should`, `always`, or `never`; first establish the intended meaning.

Support material factual claims with available evidence. Distinguish observation, inference, proposals, and uncertainty. Report what remains unverified without turning unavailable evidence into a negative finding.

Use dates, versions, or defined states when relative wording would become misleading. Preserve lifecycle terms when they name actual workflow states.

## Editorial Review

Give each paragraph a coherent purpose. Remove repetition without separating necessary context from the action. Check nearby text and cross-file terminology for semantic drift.

Preserve quotations and attributed wording. Avoid unsupported guarantees and promotional claims. Do not rewrite commands or technical examples merely to improve prose style.

## Further Reference

These principles select language guidance from the [Google developer documentation style guide](https://developers.google.com/style). Consult it for unresolved language questions; use the [Microsoft Writing Style Guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/welcome/) for relevant technical terminology and the [Apple Style Guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/applestyleguide/welcome/web) for Apple-specific interfaces.

These sources are editorial references, not compliance targets. Do not import their branding, regional spelling, heading capitalization, or publishing conventions into another project.
